Companion Analysis — Testing Tesla's Claims Against Real Physics: The Moon, Its Energy, and the Search for a Rigorous "Human Energy"

This companion analysis holds two of Tesla's own claims to the same standard: his 1919 assertion that the Moon possesses no real axial rotational energy, and his 1900 assertion that human civilizational advancement can be measured by an equation borrowed from kinetic energy. Both claims share a structure worth testing rigorously rather than dismissing outright — each proposes that a real-looking mathematical quantity actually describes something physically real. The Moon case turns out to be checkable directly: real, independent, physical measurements (laser-tracked libration, measured tidal heat, ongoing orbital recession) settle it decisively, and Tesla's own numbers, read carefully, already contradict his stated conclusion.

The second half of this document extends that same rigor outward in two directions from the settled Moon result. First, practically: once the Moon's rotational energy is established as real rather than illusory, what, if anything, could actually be extracted from it, and at what scale — a question answered here with real engineering numbers rather than speculation, and one that leads naturally into asking what happens to that same energy under the universe's own long-term fate, tested model by model rather than assumed. Second, analogically: Tesla's 1900 essay proposes a "hypothetical velocity" for humanity using the identical mathematical form he later applied to the Moon. Testing that claim with the same rigor — real biomass data, real energy flows, the same equation solved honestly — produces a genuine, defensible quantity in its own right (a unit we call the Viva), while also exposing precisely where Tesla's original civilizational metaphor was never actually claiming to be physics at all.

Throughout, the standard applied is the same one settling the Moon dispute: does a quantity have an actual, checkable physical referent, or is it real-sounding vocabulary standing in for something that was never operationally defined? Every section below is organized around answering that question directly, for whichever claim it's examining.

Contradictions

The core self-contradiction, restated at its sharpest: the follow-up article proves the ball-and-spoke system contains only energy E (translational), "no power whatever being consumed in the supposed axial rotation" — then, in its own final paragraph, computes a specific nonzero value for that same energy (1/160,000 of orbital energy). You cannot assert a quantity is exactly zero and then state what fraction of the total it represents.

Fig. 4 vs. the Moon conclusion. Fig. 4's own caption states the general rule correctly: total KE = translational (about O) plus rotational (about C) — a sum of two terms, not a substitution. His Moon conclusion collapses this to translational-only, contradicting the general theorem he just taught two figures earlier in the same article.

"Not a matter of definition" vs. extensive definitional maneuvering. He insists in the follow-up piece that his view is "NOT BASED ON A THEORY but on facts demonstrable by experiment... not a matter of definition" — immediately after spending the bulk of both articles doing exactly that: redefining what should count as "real" axial rotation (requiring an independent second rotation on top of the automatic one).

"Perfect sphere" claim vs. his own coincidence argument. The new quote — a perfect sphere could rotate exactly this way "forever" with no external forcing needed — directly undercuts his own Passage 1 argument that an exact, permanent match between axial and orbital periods with no forcing mechanism is "immensely improbable," a coincidence bordering on "miraculous." He can't have it both ways: either the match needs a mechanism (his earlier point, correctly made) or it doesn't (this quote) — not both.

"Uncertain" vs. "certitude" in the same paragraph. The passage about radius of gyration says the value is uncertain since lunar internal density distribution is unknown — then immediately states it "may be concluded with certitude" that the Moon is devoid of axial momentum. Errors

The core substitution error (covered at length already): "no additional torque needed to sustain motion already present" is quietly treated as equivalent to "no energy exists in that motion at all." These are different claims; only the first is true.

The rigid-linkage model doesn't match the actual physical mechanism. His balls-on-spokes and rifle-barrel analogies assume a mechanical/rigid coupling. The real coupling is a gravity-gradient torque acting on the Moon's slight permanent mass asymmetry — a genuinely different physical mechanism that behaves differently (see below).

The "perfect sphere" claim is independently, directly falsifiable, via the shell theorem: a perfectly spherical mass distribution feels zero external gravitational torque, at any orientation, forever. A torque-free sphere starting with any spin state (including none, relative to the stars) keeps that state unchanged — it would show Earth every face in turn over an orbit, not the same face continuously. This is checkable without touching the energy argument at all.

Fig. 8's own setup smuggles in the rotation it claims to disprove. Assigning two masses different velocities because they sit at different radii, then joining them, already encodes real rotation about their common center — "no new energy at the joining instant" doesn't mean "no rotational energy," it means the energy was already paid for in how the velocities were chosen.

The Direct Overview

Does the Moon have rotational energy? Yes — a small, definite, positive amount, currently around 3×10²³ joules, confirmed multiple independent ways (physical libration measurements, ongoing measured orbital recession, the parallel-axis theorem applied correctly to the real, slightly asymmetric Moon).

The "dark side," addressed head-on — and there's a terminology mix-up worth clearing up first. The Moon doesn't have a permanently dark side at all — every part of its surface gets roughly two weeks of sunlight and two weeks of night per lunar cycle, the far side included (that's a common popular misconception). What Tesla meant is the far side — the hemisphere permanently hidden from Earth.

Here's the resolution to the apparent paradox: having real rotational energy about its own axis is exactly what makes the far side stay hidden — not what would expose it. The Moon's spin is locked to precisely match its orbital rate, so it always presents the same face to Earth while genuinely rotating once per orbit relative to the distant stars. If the Moon had zero real rotation (Tesla's claim), it would show Earth every side in turn over the course of an orbit — the far side would become visible periodically. The real spin is the thing keeping it hidden, not the thing that would reveal it — the same logic as always facing a friend while walking a circle around them: genuinely turning relative to the room, and that turning is exactly what keeps the same side facing them.

How could energy actually be extracted, and does the answer depend on where you're observing from? This splits into two genuinely different things:

The magnitude of "kinetic energy" you'd calculate is frame-dependent — this is true of all kinetic energy, not something unique to this puzzle. Calculated relative to Earth (co-rotating with the orbit), the Moon has zero rotational KE, because in that frame it isn't spinning at all. Calculated relative to the distant stars, it has the real, positive value above.

But actually extractable, usable energy is tied to the real physical dissipation mechanism, not to which frame you choose to do arithmetic in. The mechanism is real and already operating today: tidal friction — flexing inside the Moon and Earth as the misaligned bulge drags against the mismatch between spin and orbital motion — genuinely converts some of this system's mechanical energy into heat, continuously, right now, independent of any observer's chosen frame.

To engineer deliberate extraction, you'd need to tap into that same real, physical coupling — for instance, a system referenced to inertial space (gyroscopically, not to Earth) sitting on the Moon's surface could in principle detect and harness the real ~27.3-day rotation relative to the stars; a system built only with reference to Earth's own frame would see nothing to extract, because in that frame there's genuinely nothing there.

So the "right" answer to where you'd need to be does depend on your reference frame in the sense that it determines whether you can even detect the motion to begin with — but it doesn't mean the energy's physical reality is up for grabs by choice of frame. The heat is being generated somewhere real, right now, regardless of who's watching or from where.

A Practical Elaboration: Building This With Today's Hardware
The core idea is straightforward once you separate the two reference frames properly: mount a device on the Moon's surface that ignores the Moon entirely as its reference and instead tracks its orientation against inertial space (the distant stars, or equivalently, a gyroscope's own conserved spin axis) — that device would see the Moon's surface slowly rotate underneath it once per 27.3 days, exactly the motion Earth-referenced observation hides.

A fiber-optic gyroscope (FOG) or ring laser gyroscope — both mature, off-the-shelf technologies already used in spacecraft and submarine inertial navigation today — could directly register this, since they measure rotation relative to inertial space by design, not relative to any local landmark; no exotic new physics is needed, just deploying instruments that already exist for a different purpose.

A star tracker (also standard spacecraft hardware) mounted on a lunar lander would show the same thing more intuitively — a fixed star slowly sweeping across its field of view over weeks, at a predictable, calculable rate, directly confirming the real rotation Tesla denied.

For actual energy extraction rather than mere detection, the natural approach borrows a page from tidal and Foucault-pendulum engineering: a large, torque-free flywheel or pendulum system, isolated from the Moon's own slow rotation via a gyroscopically-stabilized gimbal (the same "keep facing a fixed external direction regardless of what's underneath you" principle used in ship stabilizers and spacecraft reaction wheels), would maintain its orientation relative to the stars while the Moon's surface — and the mounting structure bolted to it — physically rotated underneath over the course of a month.

The resulting slow relative motion between the fixed flywheel and the rotating mount could, in principle, drive a generator the same way a tide-mill extracts energy from the relative motion between the (comparatively still) ocean bulge and the (rotating) Earth beneath it — the practical yield would be tiny, given how slow 1 rotation per 27.3 days actually is, but the engineering path (inertially-referenced gyrostabilized platform, geared to a slowly counter-rotating anchor point) is buildable with entirely current technology, unlike the far larger, still purely theoretical proposals for tapping Earth's own tidal dissipation at planetary scale.

The Governing Formula, and Why It's Brutal Here

Power extracted from a rotating system is P = τ·ω — torque times angular velocity. The problem isn't torque; it's ω. The Moon's rotation relative to the stars is one full turn per 27.32 days, which works out to:

ω ≈ 2.66 × 10⁻⁶ radians per second

That's an almost unimaginably slow angular rate — for comparison, a small watch's hour hand moves at roughly 1.45 × 10⁻⁴ rad/s, so this is about 50 times slower than a clock's hour hand. Since power scales directly with ω, and ω here is catastrophically small, you need an enormous torque just to get a trivial amount of power out.

A modest, buildable system lands in the microwatt-to-milliwatt range.

Piezoelectric harvesting embedded in a structure to catch that local strain energy is a real, already-studied category of energy harvesting on Earth (used for microwatt-to-milliwatt-scale sensor power in structural monitoring), and it would be tapping a genuinely different, already-confirmed energy flow rather than the astronomically slow whole-body spin. It would still land in the microwatt-to-low-milliwatt range — not remotely competitive with solar panels or the fission surface power systems actually being developed for real lunar bases — but it's a legitimately more productive place to point "harvest lunar rotational effects" ambitions than the inertial-frame flywheel idea, since the underlying process is at least locally concentrated rather than diffused across a month-long cycle.

What the Barycenter Actually Is — And a Genuinely Interesting Fact About It

First, the necessary clarification: the barycenter isn't a physical object or an energy reservoir — it's a mathematical point, the mass-weighted average position of Earth and Moon. There's no "stuff" sitting there; it's a bookkeeping location, the same way "the center of a donut" is a real, calculable point that happens to be sitting in empty air.

But here's the genuinely interesting part, worth working through carefully because it's a real, correct piece of geometry: the barycenter always lies on the line connecting Earth's center to the Moon's center, at roughly 2,900 miles from Earth's center (his figure holds up well against the modern value). That line's direction only changes slowly — once per lunar month, as the Moon orbits. But Earth's own solid body spins underneath that line once per day. So relative to Earth's rotating interior, the barycenter isn't a fixed point at all — it traces a circle, roughly 2,900 miles in radius, sweeping all the way around Earth's interior once every sidereal day. That's a real, correct, slightly delightful geometric fact.

Why That Sweep Doesn't Itself Carry Energy

Here's the catch, though, and it's important: that circular path is a moving reference point, not a moving physical disturbance — the same way a lighthouse beam sweeps across the horizon without any actual object physically traveling around it. Nothing material or energetic is "located at the barycenter" and being dragged around Earth's interior as it spins; the barycenter's motion through Earth's body is purely a consequence of how you're doing the bookkeeping, not a physical process you could tap.

What's Actually Physically Real in That Location: Solid-Earth Body Tides

The genuine physical process operating in that general depth of Earth is body tides — the same tidal flexing mechanism that causes ocean tides, but acting on the solid rock of the mantle itself, which really does deform elastically (Earth's actual solid surface rises and falls by tens of centimeters, twice daily, from this). This is real, measured, and dissipates real energy as heat — but the measured global total is only about 80–110 gigawatts, compared to roughly 3–3.5 terawatts dissipated in the oceans. Solid-Earth dissipation is under 5% of the whole system's total — the oceans, not the mantle, do almost all the tidal work. And critically: this dissipation isn't concentrated at the barycenter's depth or location — it's distributed throughout Earth's whole solid body, since the barycenter isn't a physical stress concentrator, just a coincidentally co-located geometric point.

The Practical Engineering Wall — Much Harder Than the Lunar Case

Even setting the physics aside, there's a decisive practical problem: the barycenter sits about 2,900 miles from Earth's center — meaning roughly 1,060 miles beneath the surface, deep in the lower mantle. For comparison, the deepest hole humans have ever drilled (the Kola Superdeep Borehole) reached about 7.5 miles — we've penetrated roughly 0.7% of the distance needed. At that depth, temperatures run into the thousands of degrees Celsius and pressures into the millions of atmospheres — conditions no known material or drilling technology can survive, let alone any known utility extraction device. This isn't "hard, push it out a few decades" the way lunar surface hardware is — it's a genuine hard wall with nothing on any foreseeable engineering roadmap that gets close.

The idea of computing a total angular momentum or energy via a mathematical surface "at infinity." That's a real General Relativity technique (Komar and ADM mass/angular momentum), but it comes with a strict precondition worth stating clearly: it only works for an isolated system sitting in an otherwise empty, asymptotically flat spacetime — a single star, a black hole, a solar system, with genuinely empty space stretching away from it in all directions.

On Actually Quantifying "Human Energy"

There's a real candidate hiding in plain sight if the net is cast wide enough: global Net Primary Productivity. Combined terrestrial and oceanic NPP runs to roughly 125 petagrams of carbon per year — 125×10¹⁵ g/yr. Using biomass's typical energy content (~40 kJ per gram of fixed carbon):

125×10¹⁵ g/yr × 4×10⁴ J/g = 5×10²¹ J/yr

Divided by the seconds in a year (3.156×10⁷ s):

5×10²¹ J / 3.156×10⁷ s ≈ 1.58×10¹⁴ W ≈ 158 TW

That's a real, physical, joules-per-second number, cross-validated by satellite reflectance modeling, direct CO₂ flux towers, atmospheric mass-balance inversion, and ground biomass surveys — four independent methods converging on one figure. Extend it to everything that eats — animals, fungi, bacteria, humans — and total ecosystem respiration runs close to full gross primary productivity, roughly double NPP, putting whole-biosphere metabolic throughput near 300 TW, itself independently cross-checked by Kleiber's Law holding from bacteria to blue whales. Add humanity's own metered ~18–20 TW, and the total sits at a few hundred terawatts — worth its own unit, and Tesla's own vocabulary hands us one: he called kinetic energy vis viva, so we call this the Viva.

The trouble is dimensional. Tesla's formula, E = ½Mv², wants a stored energy, not a flow rate. Global standing biomass carbon is roughly 550 GtC; at a carbon fraction of 45% of dry biomass — the same fraction implicit in the 40 kJ/g-carbon conversion above (18 MJ/kg biomass ÷ 0.45 = 40 kJ/g carbon, confirming the two figures were built from one consistent ratio) — total biomass mass works out to:

M = 550 GtC / 0.45 ≈ 1.222×10¹⁵ kg

At the same 18 MJ/kg biomass energy density:

E_stock = 1.222×10¹⁵ kg × 1.8×10⁷ J/kg ≈ 2.2×10²² J

Solving Tesla's own equation for velocity, exactly as he solved it for the Moon:

v_h = √(2E/M) = √(2 × 2.2×10²² / 1.222×10¹⁵) = √(3.60×10⁷) ≈ 6,000 m/s

Now try the throughput route instead — converting the 300 TW flow into an equivalent energy via biomass residence time, cited at ~9 years for land vegetation (τ = 9 × 3.156×10⁷ s ≈ 2.84×10⁸ s):

E_turnover = P × τ = 3×10¹⁴ W × 2.84×10⁸ s ≈ 8.52×10²² J

v_h = √(2 × 8.52×10²² / 1.222×10¹⁵) = √(1.394×10⁸) ≈ 11,810 m/s

Two different velocities from the same formula is the tell. The resolution is in the definition itself: ecological turnover time is defined as energy stock over power flow, τ ≡ E_stock/P. Substitute that straight back in:

E_turnover = P × τ = P × (E_stock/P) = E_stock

Identically — not approximately. So the two calculations were never independent; they can only diverge if τ and P are measured over mismatched system boundaries, which is exactly what happened: a terrestrial-only τ (9 years, from land NPP against land biomass) was multiplied against a whole-biosphere P (300 TW, land plus ocean plus full respiration). Done consistently, the real whole-biosphere turnover works out to:

τ_corrected = E_stock/P = 2.2×10²² J / 3×10¹⁴ W ≈ 7.33×10⁷ s ≈ 2.3 years

— not 9 — and plugging that back in reproduces the original 2.2×10²² J exactly, as the algebra demands.

What survives the correction is sharper, not softer: since v_h still scales with √E_stock at fixed M, more standing biomass genuinely does yield a larger computed velocity — an old-growth forest, holding centuries of accumulated wood against a slow, land-scale turnover far longer than the biosphere-wide 2.3-year average, really does outscore a fast-cycling grassland on Tesla's own math. And that's precisely where the reconciliation runs out of road: the ecosystems that maximize this number are the ones nearest zero net growth, living off accumulated capital rather than advancing. The equation, for once, is genuinely and rigorously satisfied — one consistent carbon fraction, real mass, real energy, real velocity, every step traceable to the same assumptions — and it still can't be squared with its author's own demand to perpetually increase the energy thus defined, because the number is maximized exactly where growth stops.

The Real Boundary: Physical Throughput, Not Abstract Advancement

Worth stating explicitly, since it resolves a real ambiguity in how Tesla's original claim gets read: the Viva is a physical quantity — real biomass, real joules, real energy density — and as such it genuinely is subject to real physical conservation laws where it actually couples to them. Specifically, physical, industrial energy throughput of the kind the Viva measures can expand and dissipate as a scalar, and where that throughput manifests as literal mass redistribution on Earth's surface (mega-engineering, excavation, large-scale construction), it remains bound to the planetary flywheel's own conservation vector by that literal mass-redistribution footprint — an infinitesimal but real coupling, the same one already discussed for Earth's own moment of inertia. What does not couple to that vector is Tesla's original, broader notion of "civilizational advancement" itself — an informal, qualitative concept, no different in kind from GDP or an organizational-complexity index, with no fixed mass distribution and no operational physical definition. It can drive the design of the technology that produces real throughput, but it never itself becomes a physical vector — only its literal, measurable physical footprint does. This is the same resolution, reached independently here, that the parallel Moon-rotation critique's own vocabulary keeps landing on throughout: real, checkable quantities earn real physics; informal descriptive ones stay informal, no matter how precisely-labeled the surrounding equations look.

Cosmological Endgames — A Disfavored, Clearly Segregated Appendix

This entire section is explicitly speculative and secondary to the document's main claims. Current observational evidence (accelerating cosmic expansion, established via the 1998 Type Ia supernova surveys) favors continued expansion — the "Big Freeze" — as the mainstream baseline. Everything below is presented as a set of disfavored alternative models, included for completeness and because it bears directly on what ultimately happens to the real rotational energy this document has spent so much effort establishing.

Tesla himself held a striking cosmological position of his own — describing the universe as a machine with no beginning and no end, humanity included in that natural order rather than exempt from it — and this appendix tests that intuition against the real range of modern candidates for the universe's ultimate fate. The natural entry point is already sitting earlier in this investigation: the atmospheric-resonance mechanism governing Earth's own day-length history is itself a story about a system finding a temporary balance point between two competing torques before eventually breaking free of it and continuing its long-term evolution — precisely the same shape of question as asking whether the universe's own expansion is a similarly temporary phase, or the genuine, permanent endpoint.

Chasing that thread seriously means resolving a real fork Tesla's "never began, never ends" quote sits directly on top of: does a genuinely eternal universe repeat by the old system ending — dissolving entirely, with a new, structurally similar system then forming in its place — or by the same system persisting continuously, merely reversing direction without ever truly ending at all? These are not two descriptions of one idea; they are the actual dividing line between the real candidate models.

Roger Penrose's Conformal Cyclic Cosmology (CCC) sits squarely in the first category: each aeon expands until its full physical content is consumed or dispersed into diffuse radiation, and a conformal rescaling reinterprets that dissolved endpoint as the smooth starting condition of an entirely new aeon — nothing physically turns around, and nothing from the old aeon is literally reused, only the category of process repeats. CCC's claimed observational signature — faint concentric rings in the cosmic microwave background, interpreted as imprints from black hole collisions in a previous aeon — has not held up under independent statistical scrutiny, and must be treated strictly as a disfavored, speculative claim rather than settled evidence.

The classical oscillating "Big Crunch" model (dating to Richard Tolman's 1930s work) and loop quantum cosmology's "Big Bounce" sit in the second category instead: the same universe, the same continuous history, genuinely reversing from contraction into expansion without ever actually ending. Notably, this is precisely why the older oscillating models fell out of favor and Penrose felt compelled to invent the dissolution-and-reset mechanism in the first place: a purely continuous bounce accumulates entropy from cycle to cycle with nothing to reset it, growing each successive cycle larger and hotter rather than truly repeating — CCC's dissolution step exists specifically to solve the entropy-accumulation problem that plagues naive bounce models.

A third, related variant worth naming: the ekpyrotic cyclic model (Steinhardt and Turok) proposes the same underlying branes colliding repeatedly, continuous rather than dissolving — but each cycle's enormous expansion phase dilutes the accumulated entropy rather than resetting it outright, a genuine hybrid sitting between the two poles: continuous substrate, non-identical repeated states. A fourth, structurally distinct possibility is branching rather than succession — eternal inflation (Andrei Linde, building on Alan Guth's original model) and the string theory landscape (associated with Leonard Susskind) don't describe one system ending or turning around at all, but countless causally disconnected regions forming simultaneously and continuously, related by shared underlying law rather than by sequence in time. And for the strictest, most literal version of "returns to an identical state," there's Poincaré recurrence: a rigorous 1890 theorem stating that any bounded system will, given sufficient time, return arbitrarily close to a prior state, a mechanism some cosmologists (notably Dyson, Kleban, and Susskind, in their 2002 work on the thermodynamics of a universe with a positive cosmological constant) have taken seriously as a genuine long-timescale feature of an eternally expanding, thermal de Sitter universe — the one place in this whole list where an actual return to a truly identical configuration is a live, technical claim rather than a loose figure of speech.

Set against all of this, the mainstream picture given current data remains heat death/the "Big Freeze" — rooted in 19th-century thermodynamics (Kelvin, Clausius) and re-derived for an accelerating universe — with the "Big Rip" (Robert Caldwell and collaborators, 2003) as the more exotic alternative if "dark energy" (the standard label for whatever is driving the observed acceleration — its actual underlying physical nature remains genuinely unresolved, not a settled quantity) grows in strength over time; neither of these repeats at all, cyclically or otherwise, and both stand as the honest baseline any cyclic proposal has to argue against.

What Any of This Would Mean for the Moon's Own Rotational Energy

Gravitationally bound systems don't participate in ordinary cosmic expansion — the mechanism has to reach down and act on a system like the Earth-Moon pair directly, and each model does this differently or not at all. Under heat death and eternal inflation, the Earth-Moon pair's tidal story simply continues, undisturbed by the larger cosmological narrative. Under the Big Rip, the expanding repulsion eventually and directly unbinds the system itself at a specific, calculable moment — a violent truncation of the rotational story rather than a smooth approach to any equilibrium. Under both CCC and the classical Big Crunch, the Moon ceases to exist as a coherent rotating body well before either model's headline event — through matter decay in one case, radiation-bath incineration in the other — so the question of its axial momentum simply stops having a referent. Under the Big Bounce and ekpyrotic models, an entirely new planet-moon pair could, in principle, later form from scratch via ordinary accretion in a subsequent expansion phase — an echo of the physics, not a continuation of this Moon's specific history. And under Poincaré recurrence, the theorem's sheer generality technically permits, at timescales with no practical physical meaning, a statistical fluctuation reproducing something resembling the present configuration — the one place in this entire list where the literal return of this particular system is even theoretically on the table.

Therefore boundary condition (i) (v_local,cm = 0) and the circular-orbit balance of gravity and centrifugal terms hold exactly only for e = 0, and hold at O(e) otherwise. The instantaneous eccentricity varies between about 0.026 and 0.077 because of solar perturbations, so these amplitudes vary by the same factor of about three. The expressions above were checked numerically against Kepler's equation for e = 0.0257, 0.0549 and 0.0770. 📌 Claude's Style Guardrails (Not Open Tasks — Precision Notes for Future Edits)

If the two-velocities/turnover-correction passage is ever reworded, it's the corrected energy value (2.2×10²² J) that resolves to one number — the two velocities themselves (6,000 m/s and 11,810 m/s) never merge. Keep that distinction explicit.

The "Real Boundary" subsection's current wording states the abstract civilizational metric has "no fixed mass distribution" — this is not the same claim as "zero momentum," and the difference matters, not interchangeable phrasing. A quantity with no defined mass doesn't have a momentum value to evaluate as zero — the concept simply doesn't apply to it at all, the same way asking for the "momentum" of a mood or a rumor isn't a question with a numerical answer. If a future edit adds explicit momentum language here, it should read "the concept of momentum doesn't apply here at all" rather than "momentum evaluates to zero" — the second phrasing would accidentally imply the metric has some physical standing (mass, however small) rather than none.

When a derivation depends on an input that's itself uncertain or actively debated, say so in the same sentence as the result — not in a separate caveat the reader can skip. And be clear that flagging the uncertainty in the input is not the same as doubting the underlying mechanism is real: the tidal phase-lag debate is about how fast rock deforms, not whether it deforms; the greenhouse uncertainty is about the exact gas mix, not whether the resonance happened; the Viva's turnover-time uncertainty is about the precise number, not whether real biomass throughput exists. An uncertain estimate of a real thing is not the same claim as an uncertain thing.
