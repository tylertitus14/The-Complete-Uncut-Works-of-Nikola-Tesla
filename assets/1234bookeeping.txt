==============================================================================
                           PROMOTING THE BOOKKEEPER
==============================================================================
How the electric potential can be something real, and what that does for
Tesla's terrestrial wave law

Working note, 1 November 2026, revised 3 November 2026. From the project "The
Complete Uncut Works of Nikola Tesla" (github.com/tylertitus14). Drafted with
Claude (Anthropic). This note will be replaced by the full wave-law
specification once that work is finished.


STATUS: WHAT THIS NOTE IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
---------------------------------------------

This is a formal model. It sets out a construction, motivated by Tesla's
ideas, that keeps Maxwell's equations exactly as they are and adds one new
kind of coupling at one clearly marked point. Some parts are not yet complete,
in particular how a receiver takes energy and how it pushes back on the
source; section 10 lists what is open.

"Physics as it stands" means three things here: classical electrodynamics
(Maxwell's equations together with the force law for charges), its quantum
form (quantum electrodynamics, part of the Standard Model of particle
physics), and special relativity. All three have passed every experimental
test so far, apart from a few disputed results discussed in section 10.

The marked point is one new kind of coupling (section 5). In this note, a
receiver is any instrument that picks up electrical effects at a distance: a
radio, a voltmeter, an oscilloscope probe, or one of Tesla's tuned terminals.
A receiver could respond directly to the electric potential, specifically the
instant potential described in section 2, and not only to the electric and
magnetic fields. How strongly it does so is described by β (beta), which can
differ from one receiver to another and from one frequency to another (section
5 gives the exact definition). Standard physics rules this out on two
principles. One is gauge invariance: many different sets of potentials
describe the same situation equally well, and nothing measurable may depend on
which set is used. The other is Lorentz invariance: no frame of reference is
special. A receiver with β other than zero would break both, because the
instant potential is defined in one chosen frame (section 5).

Scope: one Earth. The chosen frame is anchored to the Earth (section 5). The
note makes no claim about signalling between planets; extending it that way is
an open question (sections 8 and 10).

That would extend physics, not overturn it. Maxwell's equations, conservation
of charge, and the total accounting of energy all stay as they are.

β is a property of each receiver as installed, like a microphone's ability to
hear ultrasound: always present, but noticeable only when there is something
extra to hear.

The "something extra" comes from the source, meaning the transmitter,
lightning stroke, or other place where charges are being moved. It exists only
when those charges change quickly: the change (for example, a current
switching on in a transmitter, or a lightning stroke carrying charge from
cloud to ground) must be over in less time than light takes to travel from the
source to the receiver. Light needs about 3 nanoseconds to travel one metre,
and about 3 milliseconds to travel 1,000 km.

  - If the source changes slowly compared with light's travel time to the
    receiver, as nearly all everyday electrical equipment does, a receiver
    with β other than zero gives the same reading as a receiver with β = 0.
    The slower the change, the closer the agreement.
  - If the source's change is over in less than light's travel time to the
    receiver, both receivers respond when the ordinary fields arrive at light
    speed. The receiver with β other than zero also shows an extra, earlier
    response, before the ordinary fields arrive.

A β test needs two halves: a source whose change of charge is over before
light could reach the receiver, and a receiver watched closely enough to catch
an earlier response. The source can be built on purpose (a fast pulse
generator, as in the bench test of section 9) or occur naturally (a lightning
stroke, as in the lightning station of section 9). Fast sources of both kinds
are common, and everyday receivers pick up their ordinary fields all the time.
Apart from a few experiments, nobody has checked the readings of everyday
receivers, such as radios, voltmeters and oscilloscope probes, for an earlier
response before the ordinary fields arrive. Those few experiments used
purpose-built sources and ordinary laboratory receivers, and they report
anomalies, but their results are disputed (section 10). So the β of everyday
receivers is assumed to be zero, but it has not been measured in any
undisputed way.

Tesla's law needs a second claim. The construction alone does not give Tesla's
timing law. With Maxwell's equations unchanged, the Earth's own charges move
the ordinary way, and nothing then makes the instant potential peak at Tesla's
times. His law follows only if, in addition, the Earth's charge moves the way
he pictured it: seen from outside, like a front running down the Earth's axis
at light speed (section 7). So the note splits Tesla's claim into two: a
receiver claim (some receivers have β other than zero) and a source claim (the
Earth's charge moves in his axial pattern). Together they reproduce his law.

What would be new is one extra kind of interaction (receivers responding to
the instant potential) and one preferred frame. Since both touch foundational
principles, it would be a major extension, not a small tweak.

It is not a claim that the effect has been observed. Whether the world works
this way is a separate question, and it is kept separate on purpose.

Terms are defined as they come up. Where a statement is Tesla's, it is quoted
and dated. Where it is standard physics, it says so. Where it is our own step,
it says that too.


1. THE PUZZLE IN ONE PARAGRAPH
------------------------------

Electricity can be described two ways.

  - Fields. The electric and magnetic fields are what push on charges and what
    receivers respond to.
  - Potentials. The electric potential (the "voltage" at a place) and its
    magnetic partner, the vector potential, are a more compact description.
    The fields are worked out from them.

The catch is that the potentials are not unique. Many different sets of
potentials give exactly the same fields. Physicists call this freedom gauge:
choosing a gauge is choosing one set of potentials out of the many that all
give the same fields. Because every choice gives the same fields, textbook
physics treats the potentials as bookkeeping, a convenient ledger rather than
a thing in the world.

John Bell coined a word for the things a theory says really exist: beables (as
opposed to mere "observables"). The question of this note is whether the
potential can be promoted from bookkeeper to beable, and if so, what changes.


2. HOW FREE IS THE BOOKKEEPING? VERY.
-------------------------------------

The freedom is not a small technicality. J. D. Jackson (2002) described a
whole family of choices, the velocity gauge, in which the potential spreads
out from a moving charge at any speed you like: slower than light, at light
speed, faster, or instantly. Two members of the family are the textbook
standards:

  - Lorenz gauge: the potential spreads at the speed of light.
  - Coulomb gauge: the potential is set instantly everywhere by where the
    charges are right now (the ordinary electrostatic potential, applied
    moment by moment). This was Maxwell's own choice: he proposed the
    condition behind it, div A = 0 (Jackson and Okun 2001).

Every member of the family gives the same, perfectly causal, light-speed
fields. (Strictly, it is the scalar potential that spreads at the chosen
speed; the vector potential then takes a mixed form.) Jackson uses the family
to make the point that the potentials are arbitrary while the fields stay
causal. In the Coulomb gauge, for example, the instant part of the potential
is cancelled exactly by a matching part of the vector potential, so it never
shows up in the fields.

So in ordinary physics an "instant potential" is a feature of one bookkeeping
style, invisible to every receiver.


3. WHICH WAY THE FREEDOM RUNS
-----------------------------

A common first guess is that many different electric and magnetic fields can
share the same potential. For the full potential it is the other way round.
For the scalar potential alone, the guess is right. Both directions matter
here.

Potentials to fields: always exactly one answer
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The fields are worked out from the potentials by a fixed recipe:

  - E = −(slope of the scalar potential Φ) − (rate of change of the vector
    potential A), written E = −∇Φ − ∂A/∂t
  - B = (the curl, or "swirl," of A), written B = ∇×A

Hand over Φ and A, and there is one and only one E and one B.

Fields to potentials: infinitely many answers, and a way to list them all
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Going backwards, from given fields to potentials, has endless answers, and all
of them can be listed. Start from any one set of potentials that gives the
fields (the technical box at the end of this section gives three standard
recipes). Then choose any smooth function of place and time, call it χ (chi):

  - new Φ = old Φ − (rate of change of χ)
  - new A = old A + (slope of χ)

This always gives the same fields. In a region without holes, every valid set
of potentials arises this way, and only these do. (If the region has holes, as
around the shielded magnet in the Aharonov–Bohm experiment, the loop totals of
A around the holes are an extra fixed quantity.) So "all valid potentials for
given fields" is the same thing as "all smooth functions χ": one free function
of space and time.

A small example. A uniform magnetic field B pointing up (along z) can come
from A = ½B(−y, x, 0) or from A = B(0, x, 0). These look quite different but
give the same field. They differ by the slope of χ = ½Bxy.

The case where the first guess is right: knowing only the scalar potential
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

If all you know is the scalar potential Φ (the voltage at each place), then A
is unknown, and many different E and B do share it.

In our construction the real potential is the instant potential, and this case
is exactly what the extra, potential-reading part of a receiver faces (the β
part; see section 5). Here is precisely what the instant potential pins down
and what it leaves open:

  - It fixes the charge. The charge density follows from it by Poisson's
    equation, ∇²Φ = −ρ/ε₀.
  - It fixes the charge-bound part of the electric field. Split any electric
    field into a part that spreads out from charges (no swirl) and a part that
    only swirls (no spreading). This split is called the Helmholtz
    decomposition. For fields that die away far from their sources it is
    unique; inside a bounded region it also depends on the conditions at the
    boundary. The instant potential fixes the first part exactly:
    E_bound = −∇Φ.
  - It leaves the rest to Maxwell's equations. The swirling electric part and
    the whole magnetic field are whatever Maxwell's equations give for the
    currents and any incoming waves. This swirling part includes radiation
    (light, radio, Hertz waves) and also induction fields: the non-radiating
    swirl around changing currents, as in a transformer.

So every E and B with the same instant potential = the same charge-bound part
+ a swirling part allowed by Maxwell's equations. (The currents are not
entirely free: the part of the current that piles up charge is fixed by how
the charge changes.)

What this means for the Tesla model. The instant potential carries only the
charge-bound part of the field. Under the definition of β in section 5, a
receiver responds to the swirling part of the field at (1 − β) times normal
strength: fully at β = 0, not at all at β = 1. Hertz waves belong to the
swirling part, so this lines up with Tesla's repeated split between "Hertz
waves," which radiate away and are "hopelessly lost" (1916), and the earth
current, which "is stored and completely recoverable" (1916). Two cautions:
the swirling part also includes induction, which Tesla's own coils rely on;
and this is a consequence of the model, not something tested.

Technical box: recipes and gauge rules
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Three standard recipes for a first set of potentials:

  - Straight-line recipe (the "Poincaré gauge"; Brittin, Smythe and Wyss,
    1982). Pick a reference point. Then add up the fields along the straight
    line from it to each place:
    Φ(x, t) = −∫₀¹ x·E(λx, t) dλ  and  A(x, t) = −∫₀¹ λ x×B(λx, t) dλ.
    This works for any E and B that obey Maxwell's source-free equations
    (∇·B = 0 and ∇×E = −∂B/∂t), in a region where every straight line from the
    reference point stays inside the region.
  - Instant recipe (Coulomb gauge). Φ is the electrostatic potential of the
    charges at that moment, and A is built from the swirl of B.
  - Light-speed recipe (Lorenz gauge). Both potentials are built from the
    charges and currents as they were at the moment their influence, moving at
    light speed, set out.

A gauge condition is a rule that cuts down the choice of χ:

  - The Coulomb rule leaves only χ with ∇²χ = 0. For fields that die away far
    off, that leaves χ depending on time only, which shifts Φ by the same
    amount everywhere at once. It changes no difference between two points, so
    it changes no drive in this note.
  - The Lorenz rule leaves only χ that themselves travel as waves at light
    speed.
  - Jackson's velocity gauge leaves χ travelling at the chosen speed v.

Jackson (2002) writes out the χ that converts one gauge into another.


4. WHY THE POTENTIAL DESERVES A SECOND LOOK ANYWAY
--------------------------------------------------

Three standard facts suggest the potential is more than a ledger:

  1. The Aharonov–Bohm effect (1959). Electrons passing around a shielded
     magnet are measurably affected even though they travel only where both
     fields are zero. What they respond to is built from the potential. (In
     fairness, they respond only to a loop total of it, which does not depend
     on the gauge, so this effect alone does not pick out one potential.)
  2. The energy identity in the Coulomb gauge. The total energy in an
     electromagnetic field splits exactly into two parts: half the sum, over
     all charge, of charge times the instant potential, plus the energy of the
     swirling (transverse) part of the field, which includes radiation and the
     other swirling fields. The instant potential is not just a convenience
     here. It carries a definite, exactly accounted share of the energy.
  3. The scalar part of the field in quantum electrodynamics. In the fully
     covariant version of the theory, the "longitudinal" and "scalar" parts of
     the potential never appear as free radiation, but they are what carry the
     electrical attraction and repulsion between charges. They are real enough
     to do work, but bound to the charges. (How this is divided up depends on
     the formulation used, so it is a hint, not a proof.)

None of this forces the potential to be a beable. It shows the door is not
locked.


5. THE CONSTRUCTION: THREE PARTS
--------------------------------

Our construction makes the potential real with the smallest possible change.

(a) Maxwell's equations are left exactly as they are. No new terms, no new
fields.

(b) A law picks out the real potential. The real potential is the instant
potential: the electrostatic (Coulomb) potential of all charges at the same
instant. "Instant" means simultaneous in one fixed frame, called F_E in the
rest of this note: its origin is the Earth's centre of mass, and its axes do
not rotate relative to the distant stars. (Geodesists realise such a frame in
practice as the Geocentric Celestial Reference System, GCRS.) The Earth
anchors this frame but is not at rest in it: the Earth's surface turns through
F_E at up to about 465 m/s. Simultaneity in F_E differs from simultaneity "on
the surface" by at most about 66 ns across the whole Earth, negligible against
every timing in this note, and by about 0.005 ps across a 1 m bench. The
construction uses this one frame only. (Technically, the real potentials obey
the Coulomb condition in F_E.)

A frame that rotates with the Earth, or one that follows each piece of the
Earth, would not do: neither defines a single instant across the whole Earth.

(c) One new property. β (beta) is the property of a receiver introduced in the
Status section. Here is its exact definition.

A receiver picks up a signal between two terminals, for example its raised
terminal and its ground plate. Two things could drive that signal:

  - the ordinary drive: the voltage that standard physics gives along the
    receiver's own wiring between its two terminals (the electric field added
    up along that path). It arrives at light speed.
  - the instant drive: the difference in the instant potential between the
    same two terminals. It changes the moment the source's charges move.

β sets the mix:

    reading = ordinary drive + β × (instant drive − ordinary drive)

  - β = 0: the reading is purely the ordinary drive, as in standard physics.
  - β = 1: the reading is purely the instant drive, the full version of
    Tesla's picture as modelled here.
  - In between: a blend.

The same formula, regrouped. Section 3 split the electric field into a
charge-bound part, which comes from the instant potential, and a swirling
part. Added up along the receiver's wiring, that split gives exactly:

    ordinary drive = instant drive + swirling drive

where the swirling drive is the swirling part of the field added up along the
same wiring. (In symbols: in the Coulomb gauge E = −∇Φ − ∂A/∂t with A purely
swirling, so the voltage along the wiring is the difference in Φ between the
terminals plus a term from A alone.) Putting this into the definition gives

    reading = instant drive + (1 − β) × swirling drive

In plain words: β is how much the receiver ignores the swirling part of the
field. At β = 0 it ignores none of it (standard physics); at β = 1 it ignores
all of it. Note what β does not do: it never makes the receiver respond more
than normally to the charge-bound part. This form also shows where the missing
physics sits. An ordinary piece of metal responds to the total field at each
point; something real would have to make a receiver treat the two parts
differently (section 10).

Two special cases.

  - Slow sources. When the source changes slowly compared with light's travel
    time to the receiver, the swirling drive is small and the two drives
    nearly agree, so every receiver reads nearly the same whatever its β (the
    Status section's first case).
  - The early window. Before the ordinary fields from a fast source arrive,
    the ordinary drive is zero, so the reading is simply β × instant drive.
    This is what the tests in section 9 measure.

What else is pinned down:

  - β depends on frequency, written β(f). Each receiver has its own β at each
    frequency, the way a microphone has its own sensitivity at each pitch. A
    single constant β is only the simplest case. At each frequency β has a
    size and a timing shift (a phase; in the jargon, β is a complex number).
    The plain statements below about "0 to 1" and "negative β" describe the
    simple case with no timing shift. Because a response cannot come before
    its cause (in F_E), β cannot vary with frequency in an arbitrary way;
    exactly what that implies is still to be worked out (section 10).
  - β belongs to the receiver as installed and operated: its design, its two
    terminals and the wiring between them, its grounding, its load and its
    mode. Change any of these and you have, in effect, a different receiver,
    possibly with a different β. A receiver with a switch counts as two
    receivers.
  - β is not a measurement. It is like a person's weight: the weight is a
    property, and the number on a scale is a measurement of it. A test gives a
    measured value of a receiver's β, with some uncertainty (section 9).
  - Range, in the simple case. The natural range is 0 to 1. A value above 1
    would give an early response larger than the full instant drive and would
    turn the response to the swirling part upside down; it would probably
    break the energy accounting of section 6, though that is still to be
    checked. A negative value is allowed: it would show as an early dip where
    Tesla's picture predicts an early rise.
  - Two assumptions remain, both standard and both checkable: that a receiver
    responds in proportion to the signal (doubling the signal doubles the
    response, which holds for small signals and can be tested by changing the
    pulse strength), and that it does not change during a test.
  - A requirement from existing experiments. β cannot be something every atom
    has. Atomic clocks, optical cavities and Michelson–Morley-type experiments
    rule out, to extreme precision, any preferred-frame effect in how ordinary
    atoms and light behave. So in this model β must be a property that emerges
    in particular receiver designs, while the atoms inside those receivers
    behave normally. How a design could do that is the central open question
    (section 10).

Who owns what. It is easy to think β depends on the transmission, because
frequency is a property of the signal. It does not. The receiver owns a whole
β curve; the transmission only decides which part of the curve is used, as the
light you look at decides which part of a pair of tinted glasses' colour curve
matters.

The reading combines them: (the gap, from the source and the situation) × (β
at the signal's frequencies, from the receiver). A stronger or faster
transmitter never changes β; it makes the gap bigger, which makes a small β
easier to see.

Everyday receivers are assumed to have β = 0 at every frequency (see the
Status section). They cannot tell this model from standard physics, because
the difference lives entirely in the gap. One limit is already clear: ordinary
radio and GPS receivers respond to radio waves at full strength, so a β near 1
is ruled out for them. The open question is whether any real receiver has β
other than zero at any frequency. Section 9 describes how to measure it.

That completes the construction as a rule for what receivers read. Parts (a)
and (b) change nothing anyone can measure. Part (c) is the single place where
this model and standard physics part ways. What the construction does not yet
supply is a physical law for how a receiver's charges move under the new
coupling, how energy reaches the receiver, and how the receiver pushes back on
the source (section 10).

Why a law, and not just a choice
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

There are two existing ways to try this:

  - Route 1 fixed the speed at which the potential spreads (Jackson's velocity
    gauge) and declared that potential real.
  - Route 2 took the quantity that the usual gauge condition sets to zero
    (call it C) and promoted it to a new physical field, giving a new
    longitudinal wave. This idea has a long lineage. Following the history
    Hively supplied in an appendix to Modanese (2017):
    - Fock and Podolsky (1932) wrote down the extra term in the theory but did
      not work out its consequences.
    - Ohmura (1956) added the new scalar field to Maxwell's equations and
      pointed to longitudinal photons as something to test.
    - Aharonov and Bohm (1963), in their follow-up to the 1959 paper, built
      the full revised theory, energy accounting included, while arguing over
      how observable the potentials really are.
    - Hively and Giakos (2012) used it to predict a free-space
      "scalar-longitudinal" wave: an electric field pointing along the
      direction of travel, with no magnetic field.
    - Van Vlaenderen and Waser published a widely cited version (2001–2003),
      and Modanese (2017) gave a careful covariant treatment.

    Route 2's main open problem, noted by Modanese, is that the new field can
    only be produced where charge is not conserved locally.

They turn out to be one idea. Route 1 is Route 2's field C (defined to match
the chosen speed) held at zero; the full derivation is still to be written out
(section 10). And there is a standard result that says when C stays at zero:
if charge is conserved everywhere, C can never be produced; if it starts at
zero, it stays at zero. (Taking the divergence of the extended field equations
gives a wave equation for C whose only source is non-conservation of charge.
That is why C never appears in ordinary physics.)

Tesla, as it happens, describes charge this way. In his 1916 interview he
said:

    "electricity, whatever it is, certainly it is incompressible because all
    our experiments show that." (Nikola Tesla On His Work With Alternating
    Currents, ed. L. I. Anderson, p. ~132)

We read this as saying that electricity is never created or destroyed, only
moved, so charge is conserved. That is an interpretation: "incompressible"
says something about a fluid-like substance, and it says even more than
conservation (no piling up inside the bulk; in his water-bag analogy the
elastic bag, i.e. the surface, takes up the change). The argument here needs
only the weaker part.

The argument therefore has three parts of different standing:

  1. Mathematics: if charge is conserved and C starts at zero, C stays at
     zero.
  2. The model's postulate: the potential with C = 0, in F_E, is the real one.
     A law that singles out one potential from the many is exactly what it
     means for that potential to be real.
  3. History: Tesla's remark motivates that choice.

Only the first is a theorem. And C = 0 does no observable work by itself:
everything measurable that is new sits in β.

Why the instant potential, and not some other speed
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Tesla fixes this too. Every version of his propagation law we have has the
current starting from the transmitter with infinite speed (1915, 1916; "a
theoretically infinite speed" in 1927). A potential that spread at any finite
speed would delay the start at a distance. Only the instant potential matches,
so the speed is set by the text, not chosen by us.

A counter-view, stated openly. Heras (2010) argues that only the light-speed

branch: an instant potential with one preferred frame. That is the trade, and
readers can weigh it.

What remains is β. That is not a cost. It is the claim itself, the one place
where this model makes a prediction that standard physics does not. A caution
for honesty: an ordinary voltmeter connected between a raised terminal and a
ground plate reads only the fields along its own wire in standard physics, so
the model assumes it has β = 0. The model's claim concerns a particular kind
of receiver, Tesla's tuned, resonant terminal. Section 9 names that target
receiver and its frequency band in advance, so that a null result cannot be
explained away afterwards.

One more consequence should be stated plainly. With β other than zero, signals
can move faster than light in F_E. Because there is a single preferred frame,
this does not allow a signal to arrive before it was sent. That is the
standard result for theories with one preferred frame (section 8 spells out
the assumptions and limits). Whether nature allows it is, again, a separate
question.


7. WHAT IT DOES FOR TESLA'S TERRESTRIAL WAVE LAW
------------------------------------------------

Tesla's claim
~~~~~~~~~~~~~

From 1900 to 1927 Tesla described the current from his transmitter crossing
the Earth in a strange way. Here is the 1927 statement (Telegraph and
Telephone Age, Oct 16, 1927):

    "The wave starts with a theoretically infinite speed, slowing down first
    very quickly and afterward at a lesser rate until the distance is about
    six thousand miles, when it proceeds with the speed of light. From there
    on it again increases in speed ... reaching the antipode with
    approximately infinite velocity."

He adds that the receivers are affected "as if the whole flow was confined to
the earth's axis joining the transmitter with the antipode," and that the
surface spread "is much like the passage of the moon's shadow over the globe."
His timing law amounts to:

arrival time = R (1 − cos θ) / c

Here R is the Earth's radius, θ is the angle around the globe from the
transmitter, and c is the speed of light. Put plainly, the signal reaches you
when a front moving straight down the Earth's axis at light speed passes the
point on the axis directly "under" you.

Two of his own statements seemed to pull against each other:

  - 1915 (New York Times, Oct 3): transmission "is instant."
  - 1916 (interview): "the resultant current flows continuously with the same
    velocity of light" along the axis.

What the construction gives, and what it needs
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

With Maxwell's equations unchanged, the Earth's own charges move the ordinary
way: they spread over the surface, and through the gap between the ground and
the ionosphere, at light speed or slower. Nothing in the construction then
makes the instant potential peak at Tesla's times. The waveform would have to
be computed, and there is no reason to expect his law.

Tesla's law comes out only with a second, separate claim about the source:
that the Earth's charge moves in his 1916 pattern. Stated carefully, the
source claim is that the Earth's surface charge changes in a way that, seen
from outside, is equivalent to a charge front running down the axis at light
speed. A literal charge crossing the inside of a conducting Earth is not
needed, and would be at odds with the way a conductor carries its charge on
its surface.

Grant the source claim, and look at the instant potential at a receiver on the
surface:

  - It starts changing immediately, the moment any charge moves anywhere. That
    is the "infinite speed" start, and the "instant" of 1915.
  - It reaches its peak when the front passes the receiver's projected point
    on the axis, because that is when the charge is closest to the receiver.
    That moment is exactly R (1 − cos θ) / c. That is Tesla's law, and the
    "speed of light along the axis" of 1916.

So the two statements hold at once, without choosing between them.

Two claims, tested in order. Promoting the bookkeeper therefore splits Tesla's
claim into two: the receiver claim (some receiver has β other than zero) and
the source claim (the axial-equivalent charge history). Together they
reproduce his law. They are separate ideas, but an Earth-scale timing test
checks both at once: without a receiver with β other than zero, the instant
potential cannot be seen, so its peak cannot be timed. The tests in section 9
are ordered accordingly: first establish or bound β for a named receiver with
a known source, then use that same receiver to test the source claim.

What "arrival" means. Arrival is the peak of the calculated instant drive at
the receiver's two terminals. A real receiver's output peaks later by its own
response time (its resonance, gating and integration), which has to be allowed
for separately.

Three more of Tesla's statements that now read literally
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  - "Whether this current passing through the center of the earth to the
    opposite side is real, or whether it is merely an effect of these surface
    currents, makes absolutely no difference" (1916). Outside a body, a
    suitable pattern of surface charge can give exactly the same potential as
    a charge inside it. So Tesla's sentence is true of the instant potential
    if the Earth's surface charge follows that particular pattern, which is
    the source claim above. Whether a real Earth's surface charge does so has
    to be shown, first by simulation and then by measurement; it cannot be
    assumed.
  - "Variations of potential at the earth's surface" drive the distant
    receiver (1916). Similarly, in his 1905 patent the effect is "greatest in
    a maximum and zero in a nodal region." Tesla consistently describes his
    receivers as responding to potential. That is exactly the coupling that β
    measures.
  - Hertz waves versus earth currents (1916, 1919, 1927). The instant
    potential belongs to the part of the electric field that is tied to
    charges and does not radiate away. Hertz waves belong to the swirling
    part. Tesla's sharp split between the two lines up with the standard split
    between the two parts of the field, with one caution: the swirling part
    also includes induction fields, not only radiation.

The switch between Maxwell and Tesla
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The wave-law specification uses one model with two settings, one for the
receiver and one for the source:

  - β = 0 (Maxwell): nothing arrives before the light travel time, through the
    Earth or around it.
  - β other than zero, ordinary charge motion: an early response, whose shape
    has to be computed.
  - β above zero, axial-equivalent source (Tesla): an immediate onset, then
    the peak at R (1 − cos θ) / c, i.e. 5.76 ms across the Atlantic instead of
    about 16 ms.

That difference is the measurement that would tell them apart.

Tesla's timing numbers are geometry, not measurement
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

No record of Tesla measuring the timing has been found in the materials
examined. The full Colorado Springs Notes contain no timing measurement
through the Earth, and his 1916 interview gives no measured figure. His own
wording points the same way. In the patent he "estimated" the interval at
"one-twelfth or probably 0.08484 of a second," and adds that "the wave length
can be ascertained from simple rules of geometry." In 1905 the time was
"approximately ascertained."

Every number he gives follows from just two inputs: the speed of light rounded
to 300,000 km/s, and the Earth's diameter.

Tesla's Earth frequencies: six per second, or twelve?

One possible reconciliation, not yet shown: in a 5.9 Hz quarter-wave pattern,

a different phenomenon. Tesla also described the slow limit: "With
oscillations still slower the earth, strictly speaking, will not resonate, but
simply act as a capacity."

A one-station echo test
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

It needs only one station. Send an impulse into the ground and time its return
at the same place, so no second station or shared clock is required. This was
within reach of 1899 equipment.

The 85 ms figure depends on a reflection assumption: the front must reverse at
the antipode and return along the axis. In the quarter-wave reading above, it
returns inverted.

In this construction the 85 ms return needs both claims: a receiver with β
other than zero, and the axial-equivalent source. The receivers in use today,
including those of lightning-location networks and low-frequency timing
systems, are assumed to have β = 0 and, as far as we know, have not been
checked for an earlier response, so their silence neither confirms nor refutes
the model. A null result would settle the question only for the receivers and
frequencies actually used. Section 9 lays out how to measure β directly.


8. LIGHT CONES: WHERE EACH SIGNAL LIVES IN SPACE AND TIME
---------------------------------------------------------

The picture
~~~~~~~~~~~

Draw time going up the page and space going sideways. A flash of light at one
event spreads outward at the speed of light and traces a cone opening upward:
the light cone.

  - Events inside the cone can be reached by anything slower than light.
  - Events on the cone can be reached by light.
  - Events outside it ("elsewhere") cannot be reached by anything in standard
    physics.

In ordinary electromagnetism, every effect a receiver can register lives on or
inside the light cone of its cause.

Every potential has its own cone
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Each member of Jackson's velocity-gauge family (section 2) gives the potential
its own cone, with its own slope:

Speed of the potential        Its cone, compared with the light cone
---------------------------   ------------------------------------------------
slower than light (v < c)     narrower, sitting inside the light cone
light speed (v = c, Lorenz)   the same as the light cone
faster than light (v > c)     wider, reaching outside the light cone
instant (v = ∞, Coulomb)      flattened completely into a level slice: "now,"
                              everywhere at once

In standard physics these extra cones are invisible. The parts of the
potential that live on them are cancelled exactly in the fields, so the light
cone is the only one any receiver ever sees. Once a receiver can read the
potential (β other than zero), the potential's cone becomes a real, second
causal structure.

Two cones in one world is not exotic in itself. In air, sound has a cone well
inside the light cone, and the air picks out the frame it is measured in. In
water, light's own cone is narrower than the vacuum one, which is why a fast
particle can outrun it (Cherenkov radiation). What is unusual here is a second
cone wider than light's, or, in our construction, flattened all the way to a
level slice.

Tesla's law drawn in spacetime
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Tesla's arrival times, t = R(1 − cos θ)/c, depend only on how far the receiver
sits along the Earth's axis: t = z/c, where z is its axial position. In a
spacetime picture, the set of all arrival events is therefore not a cone but a
flat, tilted sheet moving along the axis at exactly the speed of light.
Physicists call this a null plane, or light-speed plane. Tesla's "moon's
shadow" is the same object: the shadow cast by a flat front.

That sheet touches the transmitter's light cone along a single line, the one
running straight to the antipode. Everywhere else it lies outside the cone, so
the arrival is earlier than light could manage by the straight route through
the Earth. The effective straight-line speed works out to c / sin(θ/2):

Angle from transmitter   Straight-line distance / arrival time
----------------------   -------------------------------------
43.2° (Atlantic)         2.72 c
90°                      1.41 c
135°                     1.08 c
170°                     1.004 c
180° (antipode)          exactly c

So, in one sentence: Tesla's law replaces the transmitter's light cone with
the flat light-speed sheet that touches it along the line to the antipode.
This is also why he could say both that the current starts with infinite speed
and that it moves at light speed along the axis. The sheet itself moves at
light speed, but where it meets the round Earth near the transmitter, the
meeting point sweeps across the surface arbitrarily fast.

Does the shape of the "earth" matter?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Partly. The construction's timing rule does not depend on shape: given the
source claim of section 7, the instant potential at a receiver peaks when the
moving charge passes closest to it, whatever the body looks like. Only the
formula R(1 − cos θ)/c is specific to a sphere; for a flattened Earth or a
car-shaped body the same rule gives a different formula, worked out from the
actual geometry.

Resonance is different. The natural frequencies and the positions of the nodes
depend on the real shape, in standard physics and in Tesla's. Tesla relied on
this himself. In 1916 he said that because the Earth "is an oblate spheroid,"
it "makes all the difference" whether an apparatus is placed near the pole or
near the equator. In 1917 he testified that his nodes would let him "measure
it exactly within four feet," meaning the Earth's diameter.

Three surfaces for every receiver
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Putting the construction together, a receiver at angle θ has three moments,
each on its own surface in spacetime:

  1. The level slice "now" (t = 0+). The instant potential starts to change.
     Receivers with β other than zero only.
  2. The light-speed sheet (t = R(1 − cos θ)/c). The instant potential peaks,
     given the source claim of section 7. Receivers with β other than zero
     only.
  3. The light cone (t = straight-line distance / c, or longer around the
     surface). The ordinary field response begins. Seen by every receiver.

Tesla mode puts the first two before the third. The second and third meet only
at the antipode.

Does this break cause and effect?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

"Now" is not the same for every observer. Someone moving relative to F_E
slices spacetime at a tilt. To them, two "instant" events are not
simultaneous, and for some observers an instant signal appears to arrive
slightly before it was sent. A real paradox, a signal sent into its own past,
needs more than that: it needs a reply that is also faster than light, but
"instant" in a different frame, so the two legs can close a loop.

In this construction every faster-than-light link is tied to the same frame,
F_E (section 5), which has a single global time. Two assumptions make the
argument exact:

  1. Every link that uses the instant potential, and every transmitter,
     receiver and automatic relay, uses that one frame.
  2. "Instant" is the limit of very fast links; any real link or relay takes
     some positive time in F_E.

Then time in F_E never decreases along any chain of signals and increases
around any real round trip, so no loop can close. This is the general result
for theories with a single preferred frame (Liberati, Sonego and Visser 2002).
It shows that the model's assumptions are consistent about cause and effect;
it is not evidence that nature has such a frame.

The honest limit. The protection holds only while all instant links share one
frame. Two separate media moving relative to each other, each with its own
instant potential and receivers that can read both, could in principle combine
into a loop.

Tesla's talk of signalling to other planets would be exactly that case. He
wrote "Talking with the Planets" (Collier's Weekly, Feb 9, 1901). In 1905 he
said his planned power plant would strike the universe with "blows that will
wake from their slumber the sleepiest electricians, if there be any, on Venus
or Mars!" ("The Transmission of Electrical Energy Without Wires as a Means for
Furthering Peace," Electrical World and Engineer, Jan 7, 1905). If Mars had
its own instant potential tied to its own frame, and a reader at each end
could read both, an Earth–Mars exchange is the kind of two-frame link that
could close a loop.

The construction as stated covers one Earth, and makes no claim about
signalling between planets. Extending it to several worlds would need a
further rule, for example that all instant potentials share one frame (Tesla's
ether would supply one); section 10 lists the options.

What is new here, and what is not
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Fair question. Velocity-gauge cones (Jackson 2002), the general causality of a
single preferred frame (Liberati, Sonego and Visser 2002), and two-cone media
are all already in the literature. What we have not found published elsewhere
is the Tesla-specific part:

  - reading Tesla's arrival surface as the light-speed sheet tangent to the
    light cone;
  - the three-surface timeline for each receiver;
  - tying the instant potential to his "incompressible" remark and his
    "theoretically infinite speed" start;
  - the result that a receiver with β = 1 would not respond to the swirling
    part of the field at all, Hertz waves included;
  - splitting Tesla's law into a receiver claim and a source claim (section
    7).

We cannot rule out that someone has written these down before; corrections are
welcome.


9. HOW YOU COULD TEST IT
------------------------

A Tesla-style receiver (receivers and sources are defined in the Status
section) is easy to build; Tesla described his own. What nobody has checked is
whether it picks up anything an ordinary receiver can't. The check is simple
in principle: set the two side by side, start the clock on both with the same
signal, and watch for anything that arrives before light could.

A small β does not move the main arrival: the ordinary signal still comes when
it always did. It adds an early signal about β times the size of the instant
drive (section 5). So these tests are about detecting a small early signal,
not about timing the main peak more precisely.

The targets, declared in advance
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Because β can depend on the receiver and on the frequency, a null result could
always be blamed on "the wrong receiver" or "the wrong frequency." To prevent
that, the targets are named here, before any data:

  - Primary target: a linear Tesla-type receiver tested below 20 kHz, using
    lightning (Design 2). This is Tesla's own stated band for his Earth
    system: the frequency "should be smaller than twenty thousand per second"
    (US 787,412). A null result here is the headline: "this receiver has β
    below such-and-such in this band."
  - Secondary target: the Earth-resonance band, 5 to 15 Hz, tested by watching
    phase and coherence over long runs rather than single pulses. Both
    readings of Tesla's frequency (section 7) are declared in advance: odd
    multiples of about 5.9 Hz, or a series built on about 11.8 Hz. This is
    where the source claim can be tested.
  - Methods test: the bench sphere (Design 1), at hundreds of megahertz to
    gigahertz. A null result there says nothing about Tesla's bands; it tests
    the method and gives a limit in its own band.

"Linear Tesla-type" means a modern analogue, built to respond in proportion to
the signal so that β can be measured. Tesla's own receivers were threshold and
gated devices. A replica of his apparatus can be tested too, as a historical
check, but not as the β measurement.

Before any run, these are written down and fixed: the receiver's construction,
height, terminal, coil, grounding, orientation and load; the band; the
expected sign of β (positive in Tesla's direction, though either sign is
reported); the smallest β the run can detect; the analysis window; and what
result counts as a null. A later test with a different receiver is a new
hypothesis and is labelled as one.

Design 1: a metal ball on a bench (methods test)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

This is Tesla's Earth shrunk down. In 1908 he wrote that a sphere "the size of
a little marble" offers a greater impediment to a current than the whole
Earth, and "every experiment, then, which can be performed with such a small
sphere can likewise be carried out, and much more perfectly, with the immense
globe." On the bench, though, the sphere is a conductor whose charge stays on
its surface, so the bench does not test Tesla's timing law (that needs the
source claim of section 7). It tests whether a receiver responds to the
instant drive at all.

Parts
  - The "earth": a spun-aluminum sphere about 1 m across (R = 0.5 m), on an
    insulating stand. A 2 m sphere doubles every timing gap.
  - The "transmitter": a fast, low-energy pulse generator (rise time about
    0.1 ns, a few hundred volts), connected between the sphere's top point and
    a small ball held 20 cm above it. That is Tesla's layout in miniature: a
    ground connection plus a raised terminal.
  - Receivers, in pairs, at 45°, 90° and 135° around the sphere:
    - Tesla-style receiver: a small ball on a 20 cm insulated post, wired to
      the sphere's surface just below it, like his tuned receivers sitting
      between ground and a raised terminal.
    - Control receiver: a standard electric-field probe at the same spot.
  - Recording: a fast oscilloscope (6 GHz or better, or a used sampling
    scope), equal-length cables, and a trigger taken from the pulse generator.

Tesla built this kind of receiver himself. His 1901 receiver patent (US
685,953) describes two plates placed where a potential difference exists, a
clock-driven switch run in step with the transmitter and set to connect only
"at or near the maximum," and a condenser that adds up many weak impulses
before releasing them. Today that is called a gated (boxcar) integrator, the
standard tool for pulling a weak, repeating signal out of noise. It is exactly
what this test needs, with one change: set the gate inside the early window,
before light could arrive, instead of at the ordinary maximum.

What to look for. The earliest ordinary signal reaches a receiver by the
shortest path through the air around the sphere. (A straight line between two
points on the sphere runs through the metal.) For points near the surface,
that is close to the along-the-surface time. The early window runs from the
pulse to that arrival, less a margin for the post heights; it has to be
computed for the actual layout.

Receiver position   Light along the surface (roughly the end of the early
                    window)
-----------------   ----------------------------------------------------------
45°                 1.31 ns
90°                 2.62 ns
135°                3.93 ns

Controls that make the result trustworthy
  - Swap the receivers between channels and between positions.
  - Repeat with the sphere removed.
  - Put the Tesla-style receiver inside a closed metal can. In the
    construction, the can's own charges move only when the ordinary fields
    reach them, so during the early window the can cannot cancel the instant
    drive. An early signal that survives the can would be strong evidence. One
    that disappears is not clean proof of leakage, because putting the
    receiver in a can changes its installation, and so possibly its β. The
    can's nearest wall is closer to the source than the receiver, which
    shortens the early window. Wires through the can, seams and gaps must be
    controlled; a battery-powered recorder inside the can avoids cables. (Why
    the can's metal should behave like ordinary metal with β = 0 while the
    receiver does not is part of the open question of what sets β; section
    10.)

Working out a receiver's β from its reading

The receiver's β is a property of the receiver (section 5); this test gives a
measured value of it, with some uncertainty, the way a scale gives a reading
of a person's weight. It can find a receiver's β whatever its size, from 1 all
the way down, and whatever its sign, not just "yes or no." A clear null result
gives an upper limit instead: "this receiver's β is below such-and-such." It
works like this:

  1. Work out the full-strength signal. The source's charges obey ordinary
     physics, so a standard electromagnetic simulation of the bench gives how
     charge moves on the generator, the small ball and the sphere. Coulomb's
     law, applied to that charge moment by moment, gives the instant drive
     between each receiver's two terminals: what a β = 1 receiver would pick
     up. It starts at once.
  2. Measure the early part. Take the Tesla-style receiver's signal inside the
     early window, referred back to its input (that is, with its ordinary gain
     removed, found by calibrating it with an ordinary signal). Use the
     control receiver to check for leakage. In the window the ordinary drive
     is zero, so by the definition in section 5 the reading is simply β × the
     instant drive.
  3. Compare the two. The receiver's β equals the scale factor that makes the
     full-strength curve match what was measured. Half the predicted size
     means the receiver's β is 0.5; a tenth means 0.1; an inverted copy would
     mean a negative β. If β depends on frequency, the comparison is done
     frequency by frequency rather than with one scale factor. Keep the order
     of the two terminals fixed, so that a reversed lead is not mistaken for a
     negative β.

Size of the signal. Just after the pulse, before the sphere's charge has had
time to spread, the source is a small charge pair: +q on the ball and −q at
the top of the sphere, 20 cm apart. For q = 1 nC, Coulomb's law gives an
instant drive across a receiver's 20 cm post of about 3.9 V at 45°, 0.9 V at
90° and 0.5 V at 135°. (With 5 cm spacings it would be about ten times
smaller.) The waveform after that comes from the simulation in step 1. At 90°:

Receiver's β         Early signal you would see   How it would look
------------------   --------------------------   ----------------------------
1                    about 0.9 V                  impossible to miss
0.1                  about 90 mV                  obvious on a single pulse
0.01                 about 9 mV                   clear after a little
                                                  averaging
0.001                about 0.9 mV                 visible after averaging
                                                  thousands of pulses
below about 0.0001   under about 0.1 mV           lost in the noise; sets an
                                                  upper limit instead

(An earlier draft gave 13 V. That figure treated the pulse as a lone 1 nC
charge and left out the opposite charge.)

Timing. 0.1 ns is 3 cm of light path. Cable lengths, channel skew, trigger
jitter, and the gap between the generator's trigger and the actual movement of
charge all matter more than anything else at this scale, so an independent
marker of the source event and a full timing budget are needed. Averaging does
not remove errors that repeat on every pulse. The choice of frame does not
matter here: across 1 m, F_E and the lab frame differ by about 0.005 ps.

Which frequencies this measures. Nanosecond pulses on a 1 m sphere probe a
receiver's β at hundreds of megahertz to gigahertz. Because β can depend on
frequency, a null result here says nothing about Tesla's operating band (below
20 kHz) or his Earth resonance (5 to 15 Hz).

A real early signal must also have the right shape (the computed instant-drive
waveform, the same at each position once scaled), and it must survive the
controls. A leak or a wiring fault will not follow that pattern.

Effort: serious-hobby or university-lab grade. A few thousand dollars with
used equipment is an optimistic estimate.

Design 2: the whole Earth, with lightning as the transmitter (primary target)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Lightning was Tesla's own signal source in 1899. It is free, and there are
thousands of strikes a day. His receiver patents say the method serves just as
well for "terrestrial, solar, or other disturbances produced by natural
causes" (US 685,954). Lightning's strongest long-range signals lie below
20 kHz, in Tesla's stated band.

Parts
  - Strike times and locations from a lightning-location network: for example
    Blitzortung, a community network built from home receiver kits, or the
    research network WWLLN. Where available, also estimates of how much charge
    each stroke moved and over what height (its "charge moment"), which some
    research networks and extremely-low-frequency stations provide.
  - One station with two receivers on one GPS-timed recorder (GPS time stamps
    are good to well under a microsecond):
    - Tesla-style receiver: the declared linear Tesla-type receiver: a raised
      terminal, a coil, and a ground rod.
    - Control receiver: an ordinary very-low-frequency loop antenna, the kind
      lightning networks already use.

In the early window the reading is β × the instant drive. The instant drive
comes from the charge each stroke moved and the shape of its channel (peak
current alone is not enough), together with the Earth's response. A β value
also needs an uncertainty analysis that covers strike timing, multiple
strokes, the receiver's own delay (a resonant coil's output lags its input),
and day–night changes in the ionosphere. A few months of data would also test
the daily timing shift that a universal frame would predict (section 10).

Safety: record distant strikes only. Never work on a raised terminal with a
storm anywhere nearby, and ground everything properly.

Design 3: a cross-check that needs no fast timing
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The regrouped form of β (section 5) suggests a test with no nanosecond timing
at all. At one frequency, compare a receiver's response to a nearby source,
whose field at the receiver is mostly charge-bound, with its response to a
distant source, whose field is mostly swirling (radiation). For a receiver
with β, the second response is scaled by (1 − β) relative to the first; for a
β = 0 reference receiver with the same ordinary response, it is not. This
needs careful calibration of both receivers' ordinary response, but it tests
the regrouped definition directly.

Other "earths": how the numbers scale
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Because Tesla's law is geometry, it applies to any conducting body, and the
numbers scale with size. For a sphere of radius R in Tesla's model: the lowest
resonance on his odd quarter-wave rule is c/(8R); the echo rate, one per round
trip, is c/(4R); his echo from the far side returns in 4R/c; and an ordinary
light-speed signal around the surface and back takes 2πR/c. These are Tesla's
model figures, not computed resonances of a real sphere.

What a receiver's β would mean in practice
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A receiver's β never changes when the ordinary signal arrives. It sets the
size of the early signal, and size decides what it could be used for. What
matters in each case is the receiver's β at the frequency actually used.

  - Signalling only needs the signal to be noticed. A smaller β can be made up
    for: a receiver with 10 times smaller β needs about 10 times more charge
    moved by the transmitter, or about 100 times more averaging.
  - Wireless power, Tesla's real goal, needs energy actually collected. That
    is harder to model than detecting a signal: the receiver must take energy
    and push back on the source, and the construction does not yet describe
    either (section 10). So this part is conditional, and detection and power
    may turn out to need separate β values. With that caution: under the
    definition in section 5, the coupling between transmitter and receiver is
    the ordinary coupling plus β times the extra coupling the instant
    potential would add, written Δk. Δk has not been derived yet; the symbol
    names the missing quantity. Close to the transmitter (well within a
    wavelength) the instant and ordinary drives nearly agree, so Δk is small
    and β hardly matters: nearby power transfer is ordinary physics. Δk
    matters at distances comparable to the wavelength, which is Tesla's
    planet-scale case. The standard result for two coupled resonant circuits
    (Kurs et al. 2007; Karalis, Joannopoulos and Soljačić 2008) gives the best
    possible efficiency in terms of a single number,
    the total coupling × √(Q_t × Q_r), where Q_t and Q_r measure how little

For scale, resonators with Q in the hundreds to thousands are routine today
(the 2007 MIT coils were around 1,000), but Δk for a receiver thousands of
kilometres from its transmitter could be very small, which raises the bar.
High resonance can still make up for a small β, which may be why Tesla
insisted so hard on resonance and "great inertia." Note also what no row says:
in none of them are Maxwell's equations wrong. The new physics, if any, is in
how some receivers couple to the potential, not in the field equations.

What to expect
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Standard physics predicts that every receiver's β is 0. Tesla's picture, as
modelled here, needs some receiver's β to be above zero. The tests do not
favour either: they measure the named receiver's β, from 1 down to about one
ten-thousandth, at the frequencies used. Any clear value other than zero would
be new physics, and the measurement Tesla never left us. A clean zero for the
declared target would put a hard number on the claim instead of leaving it to
opinion.


10. OPEN ITEMS AND WHAT TO WORK ON NEXT
---------------------------------------

This note is a working version. Here is what is still open, grouped by what it
would change.

Settled in this version
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  - The frame is F_E, anchored to the Earth (section 5). The note's scope is
    one Earth.
  - Tesla's law needs a source claim as well as a receiver claim (section 7).
  - The test targets are declared in advance (section 9).
  - Tesla's 1934–41 particle-beam claims are treated in a separate working
    note and are not used here.

Decisions that would change this note's text
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  - One frame for everything: the open extension. The note uses F_E and covers
    one Earth. Extending it to other planets would need a further rule. The
    options:
    - one frame for the whole solar system (the barycentric frame astronomers
      already use);
    - one universal frame, for example the rest frame of the cosmic microwave
      background (CMB). Standard cosmology does not single that frame out as
      fundamental, so adopting it would be an added postulate. It predicts
      something testable: Tesla-mode timings would shift with direction by up
      to about 26 microseconds at one station (about 52 microseconds between
      opposite sides of the Earth), cycling once a day as the Earth turns.
      Under F_E there is no such daily shift, so that contrast is the test
      between the two. With a universal frame, a link quoted relative to Earth
      could run backward in universal time only above roughly 811 times light
      speed;
    - each body keeps its own frame, plus a rule that no receiver can read
      another body's frame.

    Underneath is one question: is β a fundamental law, which points to one
    universal frame, or a property of the Earth-and-receiver system, which
    points to F_E? This note takes the second view.

  - Individualization (Tesla's multi-frequency "combination-lock" receivers,
    US 723,188 and 725,605). It could lower the noise floor of the tests in
    section 9. Under the definition in section 5, a receiver with β turns down
    its response to the swirling part of the field by (1 − β) rather than
    ignoring radiated waves outright, and it still responds to the instant
    potential of every moving charge everywhere, so frequency selection would
    do most of the work of rejecting interference. Whether this connects to
    Tesla's "non-interferable" claim has to be argued, not assumed.

Open physics questions
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  - A physical law for the receiver. The construction says what a receiver
    reads, not how its charges move. Still needed: the coupling that acts on
    the receiver's charges, how energy reaches the receiver locally, how the
    receiver pushes back on the source, and how momentum balances. Until then,
    wireless power (section 9) is conditional.

  - What physically sets β, and why atoms do not have it. Tesla's patents give
    a design recipe (mirror the transmitter, move a large quantity of charge,
    smooth terminals, resonance, a careful ground, low frequency, time-gating,
    individualization, placement), but no mechanism. Each item becomes a test
    variable. Any mechanism must give a large β in a Tesla terminal while the
    atoms inside it still pass atomic-clock and cavity tests (section 5).

  - Metal and shielding. Why ordinary metal, such as the can and the sphere in
    section 9, should behave as β = 0 while a receiver does not.

  - How β can vary with frequency. A response that cannot come before its
    cause, in F_E, limits how β(f) can vary. The standard Kramers–Kronig
    relations apply to proper response functions, and β may be a ratio of two
    of them, so what exactly follows is still to be worked out.

  - Can a potential-reading coupling exist with charge conserved? The known
    routes to a potential-sensitive effect (section 5, Route 2) need charge
    that is not conserved locally, which this construction rules out.

  - Route 1 as Route 2's C held at zero. To be written out in full: the
    definition of C for the chosen speed, the extended field equations, and
    the starting conditions.

  - The source claim. Simulate whether any realistic transmitter-and-Earth
    arrangement gives the axial-equivalent surface-charge history, and compute
    what ordinary surface spreading gives instead.

  - Tesla's six and twelve (section 7): the same quantity described two ways,
    two different modes, or a revised estimate.

  - A possible bridge to known theory: Bell and Bohm's pilot-wave theory (the
    source of the word "beable") needs a hidden preferred time-slicing, and
    Valentini's "quantum non-equilibrium" is a proposal under which that
    slicing could become detectable. A preferred time-slicing by itself gives
    no coupling to the Coulomb potential or to a receiver's β, so for now the
    link is an analogy. To be checked against the primary sources before any
    claim is made.

  - Bounds on β from existing experiments. No clean limit on β exists yet.
    Static tests of electromagnetism (Coulomb's law, the photon's mass) cannot
    see β at all, because for unchanging charges the instant potential and the
    ordinary fields agree. Precision tests of Lorentz invariance rule out β as
    a property of ordinary atoms and light (section 5), but they cannot bound
    the β of a particular receiver design without a coupling law. The
    experiments that bear on β directly are timing experiments on the field
    bound to charges, and they are disputed:
    - Kholmetskii, Missevitch and colleagues (J. Appl. Phys. 2007; EPL 93,
      64004, 2011) report "anomalously small retardation of bound EM fields"
      close to their antennas, an arrival earlier than light-travel time would
      allow.
    - De Sangro, Pizzella and colleagues (Eur. Phys. J. C 75, 137, 2015)
      report results "compatible with an electric field rigidly carried by the
      beam itself." Shabad (Eur. Phys. J. C 76, 508, 2016) argues that what
      was registered was the ordinary acceleration-dependent field instead;
      the authors replied in 2017, standing by their reading.
    - Lightning-location and very-low-frequency networks are the most
      promising existing data: millions of recorded strikes, millisecond early
      windows and microsecond timing. Their noise floors could give a real
      upper limit on β for those specific receivers in their band.
    - Everyday high-speed electronics shows no early precursors, which
      suggests that ordinary probes have small β at gigahertz frequencies, but
      no published analysis turns this into a number.

    The task: for each of these, compute what β the measurement would imply or
    rule out under this model.

  - Assumptions used in this note: that receivers respond in proportion to the
    signal and do not change during a test (section 5); that the
    coupled-resonator result applies (it assumes high Q and weak coupling);
    and that the extra coupling Δk can be derived and estimated for long
    distances. Still to check: whether a β above 1 really breaks the energy
    accounting. Tesla's coherer-type receivers were strongly non-proportional,
    so the first assumption may not hold for his own devices (section 9 uses a
    linear analogue for that reason).

  - Carried over from the wave-law work:
    - What medium could make the Earth's body nearly lossless, as Tesla's
      figures require. The Earth's finite conductivity damps and delays fast
      changes, so long-distance tests have to use low frequencies.
    - Which of Tesla's loss figures to treat as the main one: 1% to keep the
      Earth vibrating (1916), at most 5% across the globe (1917), or 0.5%
      (1927).
    - Tesla accepted Hertz waves in 1916 but wrote "if they exist" in 1927.
    - Whether the axial current is real or a stand-in: now the source claim of
      section 7.
    - An optional run below the Earth's resonance, where the potential is
      nearly uniform everywhere ("simply act as a capacity," 1905).

Model and simulations
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  - Add β and the source claim to the project's transmission-line model of the
    Earth. Run onset and peak times against distance for β = 0 and β = 1, for
    ordinary surface spreading and for the axial-equivalent source, on a
    sphere and on the real, slightly flattened Earth.

  - Simulate the bench sphere: the surface-charge history after the pulse, and
    the instant drive it gives at each receiver.

  - Add the one-station echo test (section 7) as a standard case.

  - Compare shapes (sphere, flattened sphere, a car-like box) for resonance,
    node positions and timing.

  - Check that both settings reproduce what Tesla actually observed: the 25 to
    70 km stationary waves from lightning, the sparks on the power-line
    arresters, lamps lit through the ground, and the relay switching on and
    off as storms receded.

Experiments
~~~~~~~~~~~

  - Build the bench test in section 9 (methods test), using a gated integrator
    in the spirit of Tesla's own receiver.

  - Set up a lightning station (section 9, Design 2; the primary target). A

    predict.

  - A long-run phase study in the 5 to 15 Hz band (the secondary target).
